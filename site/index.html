Smart compass.
Find your friends.

We’re building a wearable medallion to help you find your friends. It’s designed to share locations by radio and use a compass to point you toward friends wearing one too. It also has a spiral of lights and an infinity-mirror effect. The active prototype is the 200 mm narrow design. It uses one removable protected XTAR 21700 cell.

The build

Light you can wear.
Direction you can share.

The planned build: a 256-LED centre display, three additional LED strips and two controller boards.

The Evil Genius Labs Fibonacci256 forms the centre spiral. The narrow design adds one inward ring with 70 LEDs and two outside rows with 70 LEDs each. The outside rows form one 140-LED chain; the assembly has 466 LEDs. Adafruit Sparkle Motion will drive the lights; an STM32 BlackPill will read the sensors and handle the LoRa radio.

Navigation. We plan a compass mode that points north and toward live peers, plus a separate map mode showing peers relative to you. The goal is to navigate and exchange live locations without a phone or internet connection.

Light patterns. We plan geometric and microphone-reactive patterns across the centre display and strips. These simulations show a rotating rainbow with an eight-second colour cycle.

I’m experimenting with light patterns over here.

Inside the narrow design, a black acrylic mirror in front of the board provides the reflective rear surface. A clear two-way mirror closes the front. The inner LED ring sits between those mirrors.

The black acrylic also absorbs light. These renders include that loss: the estimated coated black sheet transmits 9%, while the finished clear front mirror transmits 30%. Light from the narrow board passes through both sheets; the inner ring bypasses the black sheet. A mirror coating does not act as a diffuser, so the model treats diffusion separately.

These are CAD and optical simulations. The clear mirror split comes from supplier data; the coated black mirror and printed diffuser response remain estimates. Firmware, finished brightness, fit, heat and radio performance still need physical validation.

One removable cell

Captive, tool-free battery access.

Support the pendant. Put S1 fully OFF and disconnect external power. Open the captive door, hold the cell, release the independent keeper and extract the cell with its pull ribbon. Follow the permanent polarity marks when you insert one compatible protected XTAR 21700 6000 mAh button-top cell. Close the keeper and door, check the catch, select ON and press START.

OFF isolates the positive cell contact in the reviewed topology. Reset and discharge timing remain unmeasured. A rapid replacement can retain electronic state. Light output does not establish GPS, radio or navigation readiness.

Charge the removed cell off-body with a compatible 4.2 V-termination Li-ion charger. Do not charge in the pendant. Keep spare terminals individually insulated. After damage, heat or repeated darkness, stop and service the unit. Remove the cell before USB 5 V service; keep S1 OFF.

Review the narrow prototype files and print-service package, installed inventory and raw mass and balance records. The package includes fit samples, both alternative ring materials, source-linked print settings, assembly references and the full wire cut list.
